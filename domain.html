Literature survey

The survey covered smart-waste IoT systems, adaptive sampling and send-on-delta reporting, energy-aware transmission control, Age of Information (AoI), and edge computing for IoT. It was updated three times (proposal, v3 in May 2026, v4 in September 2026) to check whether newer work had closed the gap.

AreaWhat existing work doesWhat it leaves open
IoT smart binsFill-level, gas and location sensing with dashboards and route planning.Most bins send raw readings at a fixed rate; little attention to what is worth sending.
Energy-aware transmission (e.g. Giordano et al., 2023)AIMD control of the reporting interval from the battery state of charge.Not applied to waste bins, and not combined with urgency or data-age limits.
Adaptive sensing in smart bins (e.g. Ambrosia, 2021; Wu et al., 2026)Fewer transmissions and longer battery life by adapting to bin usage.Adapt to usage, not battery; no bound on how stale the server's data can become.
Send-on-delta / deadband reportingReport only when a value changes by more than a threshold.Fixed thresholds; do not tighten when a bin is filling fast.
Age of InformationMature theory for scheduling fresh updates across many sources.Rarely enforced as a hard per-state limit on a microcontroller.
Edge computing (Shi et al., 2016; Satyanarayanan, 2017)Processing near the data source reduces latency and backhaul traffic.General architecture; does not define a waste-specific decision policy.

Research gap

Each mechanism exists on its own in the literature. No reviewed 2023–2026 work combines, on one battery-powered bin node, (i) an AIMD interval driven by battery charge, (ii) a deadband that adapts to fill speed, (iii) an urgency override that always wins over energy saving, and (iv) a per-state maximum data age. The contribution is the joint policy and its evaluation on real hardware, not a new individual mechanism.

A secondary, applied-engineering contribution is a gateway that ranks a small fleet of bins by urgency and freshness and grants short "report faster" leases only when the network link is healthy.

Research problem

How can a low-cost, battery-powered smart-bin node reduce unnecessary transmissions, and the energy they cost, without delaying or missing the events that matter for collection (near-overflow, rapid filling, gas)?

Research objectives

Main objective

Design, implement and evaluate an edge-based, energy-efficient smart-waste system in which each bin node decides locally which readings to transmit.

Specific objectives

  1. Build smart-bin nodes (ESP32 and ESP8266) with fill-level, temperature/humidity, gas and power sensing.
  2. Implement a four-state policy (STABLE, ACTIVE, ALERT, CONSERVE) that jointly controls the transmission interval, deadband, urgency override and data-age floor.
  3. Build an edge gateway that validates, stores and ranks readings from several bins and serves an operator dashboard.
  4. Evaluate the policy against a fixed 60 s baseline on the same hardware: transmission count reduction, alert recall and latency, and measured energy per mode.

Methodology

Design science combined with controlled experiments.

  1. Build the artefact: node firmware, gateway, dashboard, mobile view and evaluation scripts. Bin depth is set per node with a Calibrate command, because the depth changes with every installation.
  2. Policy, every 1 s on the node: check urgency first (fill ≥ 80%, gas, or fill rate > 10 %/hr gives ALERT), then battery (CONSERVE), then activity (fill ≥ 60% gives ACTIVE), else STABLE. A reading is sent when the state's timer expires, the fill has moved by more than the current deadband, or the node enters ALERT. After each send the AIMD rule adjusts the interval from the battery level.
  3. Energy: an INA219 sensor on each node's 5 V supply measures current, power and energy. The battery percentage the policy reads is supplied by the gateway, so battery life is modelled, not measured.
  4. Evaluation: one node (bin01), one fixed 60 s BASELINE phase, and two ADAPTIVE scenarios: a quiet run with an empty bin (steady-state TCR and energy) and an alert run with a card held above 80% fill (alert recall and a stress-case TCR). Every row carries the server receive time and a run label. Every figure is labelled measured, modelled or inferred.

Results (20 October 2026, bin01)

MeasureResultBasis
Transmissions, steady state14 (BASELINE) vs 2 (ADAPTIVE) in 15 min: 85.7% reductionMeasured
Alert recall2 of 2 alert events reported within 60 s (about 1 s for the cleanly timed event), 0 false alertsMeasured
Mean node powerAbout 950 mW (199 mA) in every modeMeasured (INA219)
Stress caseTwo alert events in 20 min: 66 transmissions per hour vs 56 for BASELINE (18% more)Measured
Battery life with deep sleepAbout 7.4 times longer in steady stateModelled

What this means. The policy removes most routine messages and still reports urgent events at once. On the prototype the radio is not the main energy cost: deep sleep is off and the MQ-135 heater and the awake ESP32 dominate, so fewer messages did not lower the measured power. Turning the message saving into battery life needs deep sleep and a switched gas heater. In the small test bin one ultrasonic step is 2.7% of fill, above the 1% deadband floor, which explains the stress-case result; the fix is to set the floor above one sensor step and scale the fill-rate rule to the bin depth.
